## 1. Your AI-built app is probably serving its database to strangers

- **Working title:** The Supabase Row-Level Security Checklist for AI-Built Apps
- **Why now:** On 2026-11-13/14 Crissy Field GmbH, working with Handelsblatt, published a
  scan of 159,041 Lovable-built projects from Dec 2025–Sep 2026. Of the 34,545 using
  Supabase, **24,664 returned database rows on page load with no login** — 200,000+ chat
  messages and 1,000+ identity-document records (passport numbers, expiry, nationality).
  Root cause is the default `anon` key shipped without row-level security, not Lovable's
  own infrastructure. Hit the HN front page 2026-11-14 at ~981 points / 372 comments.
  *(Primary report host blocked here; figures consistent across Cybernews, Startuphub,
  Handelsblatt summaries — open the Crissy Field report directly when drafting.)*
- **Reader value:** A defence-only audit any solo founder can run in 30 minutes on a
  Supabase/Postgres backend: prove whether your anon key leaks, write the RLS policies,
  test them from an unauthenticated client, and the CI check that stops regressions.
  For anyone who shipped something with an AI app builder and never read the SQL.
- **Target phrase:** "supabase row level security checklist"
- **Links to:** `ai-agent-hacking-small-saas-hardening-checklist` (hub),

## 2. macOS 27.3 changes what you can grant an AI agent

- **Working title:** macOS Full Disk Access Is Splitting in Two: What Mac App Developers Must Change
- **Why now:** Apple published "Updates to Device Control and Data Access, and Full Disk
  Access in macOS" on **2026-11-16** (developer.apple.com/news/?id=oqng0zjk), shipping in
  macOS 27.3 beta 2 the same day. Device Control and Data Access split into **System & App
  Protection Override** and **Accessibility**; users can scope access to specific folders
  or apps, or make an app ask every time. Apple explicitly tells developers to adjust
  onboarding and verify their app still works under scoped access. Backup and assistive
  apps can request entitlements to keep prior behaviour. TechCrunch framed it as a response
  to AI agents asking for the keys to the machine.
- **Reader value:** What actually breaks, how to detect scoped-vs-full access at runtime,
  how to rewrite a permission onboarding screen that currently says "grant Full Disk
  Access", which entitlement to request and when you won't get it. Defence-only.
- **Target phrase:** "macos full disk access changes"
- **Links to:** `mobile-remote-coding-agent-apps-security`,
  `muse-ai-agent-hijack-privileged-assistant-checklist`,
  `developer-id-certificate-expiry-2027-mac-apps`

## 3. Two new age-rating questions went live yesterday

- **Working title:** App Store Age Rating Descriptors: The Jan 12 Deadline Every App Now Has
- **Why now:** Announced 2026-11-02, the new content descriptors went **live in App Store
  Connect on 2026-11-16** and **every app must have them declared by 2027-01-12**. The two
  are **Shared Location** (sharing real-world location with another person or group) and
  **Dating and Relationships** (connections between real people, or a virtual companion with
  romantic/sexual content). Dating and Relationships forces a **minimum 18+ rating**; in
  Brazil, Shared Location requires A16 and Dating and Relationships A18 unless you obtain an
  official MJSP rating. Verified on Apple's developer news page.
- **Reader value:** A decision tree for the two questions that catch apps nobody thinks of
  as dating or location apps — a run-tracking app with friend sharing, an AI companion
  feature bolted onto a journal app, a family-safety tool. What an 18+ rating costs you in
  discovery, and the Brazil exemption route.
- **Target phrase:** "app store age rating descriptors"
- **Links to:** `app-store-creative-assets-product-page-headers`,
  `google-play-contact-picker-location-button-deadline`,
  `app-store-review-process-what-nobody-tells-you`
