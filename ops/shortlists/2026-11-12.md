## 1. Apple's two new age-rating descriptors — the Jan 12, 2027 declaration checklist

- **Why now:** Apple announced on Nov 2, 2026 that starting **Nov 16, 2026** the App Store
  Connect age-rating questionnaire adds two required declarations — **Shared Location** and
  **Dating and Relationships** — and that **all apps must have them completed by Jan 12, 2027**.
  Declaring Dating and Relationships forces a **minimum 18+ rating**; in Brazil, Shared Location
  requires A16 and Dating and Relationships requires A18, with an appeal route via MJSP.
  Source: [Apple Developer News, Nov 2, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=yxkh08us) (opened in full).
- **Reader value:** A decision table for indie and small-team app developers: which of your
  features actually trip "Shared Location" (a group trip planner? a share-my-ETA button?),
  what the 18+ floor does to your store placement and family-sharing eligibility, how to answer
  the questionnaire without over-declaring, and what to do about Brazil. Includes the
  "declare on your next update" trap: the questionnaire is required whenever you submit an
  update, so this lands sooner than Jan 12 for most teams.
- **Target phrase:** "app store age rating descriptors"
- **Links to:** `apple-eu-unified-app-store-terms-indie-guide`,
  `google-play-contact-picker-location-button-deadline`,
  `app-store-review-process-what-nobody-tells-you`
- **Confidence:** High — primary source opened and quoted.

## 2. Apple shipped retention messages — what to put in your cancellation flow

- **Why now:** On **Nov 4, 2026** Apple made **retention messages for subscriptions** available.
  They appear inside the subscription cancellation flow, can carry text, an image or a special
  offer, and are configured in App Store Connect with subscriber eligibility rules and a wait
  time between offers. There is also a **Retention Messaging API** for choosing which message a
  given subscriber sees, available by request.
  Source: [Apple Developer News, Nov 4, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/) (opened; item dated Nov 4).
- **Reader value:** The first honest look at save-offers on iOS for solo founders: what to
  offer at cancel time (discount vs pause vs downgrade), why the eligibility + cooldown
  settings matter more than the copy, how to measure incremental saves rather than vanity
  "retention" numbers, and when a save-offer is worse than letting the user go. Pairs
  naturally with billing-side churn work.
- **Target phrase:** "app store retention messages"
- **Links to:** `sell-the-year-bill-the-month-storekit`,
  `custom-billing-engine-stripe-subscriptions`, `pricing-page-aeo-indie-saas-checklist`

## 5. Play Age Signals: handling age bands without building an age-verification system

- **Why now:** Google's **Oct 29, 2026** Play policy announcement put **Play Age Signals** in
  front of all developers alongside a November **trader-status** declaration requirement in Play

  signal is returned (the common case), what the "no analytics" term rules out of your funnel,
  how to fill the Data Safety form when the client library collects nothing, and why gating
  content on a band beats collecting birthdates yourself.
- **Target phrase:** "play age signals api"

## 1. Node.js patched 16 CVEs on Nov 11 — the upgrade-and-audit checklist

- **Why now:** **Nov 11, 2026** (yesterday) Node.js shipped security releases fixing
  **16 CVEs — 7 high, 6 medium, 3 low** — in **v22.23.5, v24.22.3 and v26.13.1**. The worst for
  a small backend is **CVE-2026-123864** (high): a zero-length TLS write re-enters the parser,
  causing a use-after-free whose **freed heap memory leaks into HTTPS response bodies**
  (cleartext `node:http` is explicitly *not* affected). Also high: two HTTP/2 use-after-frees
  (**CVE-2026-123866** sends freed memory to the peer; **CVE-2026-123874** on
  `respondWithFile()`/`respondWithFD()`), an HTTP/2 shutdown FD+memory leak
  (**CVE-2026-123865**), a remote TLS-server crash via deferred SNI/OCSP/session callbacks
  (**CVE-2026-126051**), and **CVE-2026-123872**, where a client changes servername during TLS
  1.2 renegotiation and **carries client-cert auth to a stricter host**. Medium ones that bite
  ordinary apps: **CVE-2026-123871** prints proxy credentials verbatim in
  `ERR_PROXY_INVALID_CONFIG`, and **CVE-2026-123877** makes `ca` omitted and `ca: []` share an
  `https.Agent` session-cache key. Source: [Node.js, Nov 11, 2026 security releases](https://nodejs.org/en/blog/vulnerability/november-2026-security-releases) (opened in full).
- **Reader value:** Defence-only, and unusually concrete because most of these have clear
  preconditions: a triage table of "do I actually run this code path" (do you terminate TLS in
  Node or behind a proxy? do you serve files over HTTP/2? do you use `server.addContext()` for
  per-host mTLS? do you pass a proxy URL with credentials?), the upgrade matrix for 22/24/26,
  why `ca: []` is not the same as no `ca`, and a short list of the log and error surfaces worth
  grepping after upgrading. The advisory states **no workarounds** for any of the 16 — upgrading
  is the mitigation, which is itself the story.
- **Target phrase:** "node.js security release checklist"
- **Links to:** `custom-billing-engine-stripe-subscriptions`,
  `ai-agent-hacking-small-saas-hardening-checklist`, `plugin4shell-ai-coding-agent-security`
- **Confidence:** High — primary advisory opened and every CVE above read from it. One
  correction to flag: widely-repeated secondary reporting says "Node 20 is EOL and got no
  patch". **The advisory never mentions Node 20.** It names only 26.x/24.x/22.x and says
  "End-of-Life versions are always affected when a security release occurs." If this post runs,
  Node 20's status gets sourced from the release schedule or left out — not asserted.

## 2. Your GitHub Actions secrets are the target: the `wfx` campaign is running right now

- **Why now:** A CI-secret-stealing campaign is **active as of this morning**. I verified it
  first-hand rather than taking a vendor's word: GitHub's own search returns **1,555 pull
  requests on the indicator branch `wfx-docs`, every one dated Nov 11–12, 2026**, and PRs
  carrying the campaign's title "docs: dependency audit notes" were still being created at
  **09:36 UTC today**. Vendor analyses describe a payload that dumps Actions secrets, runner
  memory, OIDC tokens and cloud credentials, **deletes the run logs and reverts the commit** so
  the branch looks clean, with reported scale ranging from ~1,800 repos where the payload
  executed to 61,764 probed. Verified via `gh api search/issues` against GitHub.
- **Reader value:** Pure defence, and the timing makes it genuinely urgent: how a probe PR from
  a forked branch ends up able to read secrets at all (`pull_request_target`, the classic
  footgun), the four-line permissions block that defuses most of this, why "we rotated the
  secrets" is insufficient when the payload plants cloud persistence, how to check your own org
  for the indicators **including the branches and deleted run logs an attacker tried to hide**,
  and the OIDC-over-long-lived-keys argument for solo maintainers who have one `AWS_ACCESS_KEY`
  in repo secrets. Includes the honest caveat below about counting.
- **Target phrase:** "github actions secrets hardening"
- **Links to:** `plugin4shell-ai-coding-agent-security`,
  `ai-agent-hacking-small-saas-hardening-checklist`,
  `developer-id-certificate-expiry-2027-mac-apps`, `mobile-remote-coding-agent-apps-security`
- **Confidence:** High on the facts I gathered myself; the vendor write-ups
  (safedep.io, aikido.dev, stepsecurity.io, endorlabs.com, helpnetsecurity.com) are **all
  unreachable from here**, so their numbers would be cited as reported, not verified. Note a
  real counting trap I hit: searching the PR *title* returns **65,967** results, but the oldest
  are innocuous PRs from 2020 and 2025 that merely share a generic title — the branch-scoped
  **1,555** is the defensible figure, and GitHub search only covers public repos' default
  branches, so the true total is higher. That distinction is worth putting in the post.

## 5. Play's February 2027 quality bar: memory, bitmaps and DEX

- **Why now:** Google's upcoming **technical quality requirements** take effect **February 2027**
  and introduce pass/fail thresholds on **memory usage (Anonymous RSS + Swap)**, **bitmap memory
  usage** and **DEX code optimization**, judged over a rolling **28-day** window on **Android
  13+** mobile and tablets. Reported thresholds: bitmap **200 MB** user-perceived / **200 MB**
  background / **400 MB** cached, RAM-tiered memory ceilings (≈2 GB foreground on a 4 GB device
  up to ≈4.25 GB on 16 GB), and **≥25%** combined optimization/shrinking/obfuscation of DEX for
  apps over **10 MB** of DEX (games over 50 MB). Missing the bar reportedly costs **store
  visibility and publishing capability**. The dated hook: the new metrics are reported to appear
  in **Play Console from November 2026**, which is the window to measure before the bar bites.
- **Reader value:** A measure-it-now post for small Android and React Native teams: where to
  find the new vitals, what Anonymous RSS + Swap actually counts (and why image caches dominate
  it in a React Native app), getting R8 past 25% without breaking reflection, and a realistic
  sequence for a team with one Android developer and four months.
- **Target phrase:** "play console technical quality requirements"
- **Links to:** `google-play-contact-picker-location-button-deadline`,
  `android-developer-verification-sept-30`, `google-play-12-tester-rule-indie`
- **Confidence:** **Low — do not pick this one without fixing source access.** Google's
  authoritative page is Play Console Help on `support.google.com`, which does not resolve here;
  `developer.android.com/google/play/technical-quality-requirements` and
  `developer.android.com/google/play` both return **404**; the Android Developers Blog post is on
